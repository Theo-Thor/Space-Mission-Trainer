# Science and fiction

Chapter 1 is the introduction. The facility part stays qualitative. The flight sequence at the end of the chapter uses a small set of real figures, each with a NASA source shown on screen.

## Fictional

- Selene Mission Preparation Facility
- The spacecraft, its boarding hatch, and its launch vehicle
- Selene Base at the lunar south pole
- The year 2048
- The crew: the player, Riley Morgan, Dr. Maya Chen, Leo Voss, Nia Brooks, Val Ortiz, and Dr. Samir Patel
- Mission Control lines in this chapter
- The spacesuit and flight readiness steps on screen
- The Moon picture in the briefing room, which is a drawn disc, not a mission map

None of these are NASA assets, flight hardware, or measurements. The flight HUD labels the mission as fictional.

## Choices made to stay accurate

- The boosters and the core stage are shown dropping away. Spent stages do not travel to the Moon.
- The Earth–Moon view is labeled "not to scale". At true scale the Moon would be a speck about 30 Earth diameters away.
- The trip timer counts up to about 3 days and states that the animation compresses it.
- At the south pole the Sun stays near the horizon, so the landing scene has a low Sun, long shadows, and vertical solar panels.
- Earth is drawn low over the horizon, as it appears from the south polar region.
- Landing dust flies out in low, straight sprays and drops. There is no air on the Moon, so it does not billow.
- The mission clock and the descent altitude/speed readouts are labelled "illustrative" on screen. They follow a typical crewed profile (about 3 days to the Moon) but are not real mission data.
- Earthrise is shown through the craft window with Earth partly lit, as in the Apollo 8 photograph.
- Space shots are nearly silent. The tones stand in for cabin and radio sound.

## What the dialogue is allowed to say

The briefing and Dr. Patel's optional conversation stay qualitative:

- Some areas near the lunar poles stay in shadow.
- Those regions may preserve water ice. The mission is to investigate evidence, not to assume a discovery.
- Lighting can differ sharply across short distances near the pole, which matters for power and route planning.

No temperatures, depths, coordinates, or ice amounts are invented.
